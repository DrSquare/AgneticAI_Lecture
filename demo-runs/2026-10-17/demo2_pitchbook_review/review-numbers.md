### Paying versus booked or contracted

September 2026 row 14 has 46 paying robots and 12 unpaid pilot robots, across 11 paying and three unpaid pilot customers.

- Paying annualized revenue = 46 x $3,500 x 12 = **$1,932,000**.
- Pilot annualized capacity = 12 x $3,500 x 12 = **$504,000**.
- Blended field = $1,932,000 + $504,000 = **$2,436,000**, which overstates paying ARR by **26.087%**.
- The data dictionary defines this blended field mechanically. It does **not** establish that unpaid pilots are signed future revenue. Contracts, collections and commitments are absent. Do not relabel it "contracted ARR."

### Growth and retention

Paying ARR grew **6.5714x** over Sep25-Sep26 (CSV rows 2 and 14). This is a **557.14% increase**, not 657.14% growth. September's MRR growth was **9.5238%** (rows 13/14). Q3 cumulative growth was **39.3939%** from June to September (rows 11/14), equivalent to **11.7072% per month geometrically**. Growth remains substantial; the metric-definition problem does not show that growth stalled.

NRR is **157.1429%** for the three customers paying in Sep25, using the cohort field, not total MRR. A three-customer sample is too narrow to establish representative fleet retention or customer churn. Customer-level expansion, contraction, logo churn and cohort composition are unavailable.

### CAC method and interval sensitivity

The skill's formula is S&M spend / (new MRR x GM). This CSV only permits **net** new MRR, not gross new customer MRR. Therefore the calculation below is a transparent aggregate proxy; expansion, churn and acquisition timing can distort its interpretation.

| Spend interval | Spend CSV rows | Baseline row / month | S&M | Ending minus baseline MRR | At Sep26 41% GM | At revenue-weighted interval GM |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sep26 | 14 | 13 / Aug26 | $170,000 | $14,000 | 29.6167 months | 29.6167 months (41.0000%) |
| Jul-Sep26 (Q3) | 12-14 | 11 / Jun26 | $490,000 | $45,500 | **26.2664 months** | 27.0843 months (39.7619%) |
| Apr-Sep26 | 9-14 | 8 / Mar26 | $910,000 | $91,000 | 24.3902 months | 26.6481 months (37.5261%) |
| Oct25-Sep26 (12 months) | 3-14 | 2 / Sep25 | $1,560,000 | $136,500 | 27.8746 months | 33.8281 months (33.7842%) |

Q3 example: ($155,000 + $165,000 + $170,000) / (($161,000 - $115,500) x .41) = **26.2664 months**. Thus the demo's expected "~26 months" is supported by the **Q3 ending-margin proxy**, not a unique result from the data. The four-month deck claim is unsupported under every listed aggregate method.

Revenue-weighted GM = sum(monthly MRR x monthly GM) / sum(monthly MRR). It weights existing revenue, not incremental MRR. An alternative that weights monthly net additions is a different method and must be labeled; do not silently mix it with this table. Do not include the Sep25 S&M row in a Sep25-to-Sep26 increment calculation: that would count 13 spending observations against a 12-month increment.

### Runway and forward burn

Current static runway: $6,038,215 / $713,990 = **8.4570 months** at Sep26 burn, before financing. Static post-raise arithmetic: ($6,038,215 + $18,000,000) / $713,990 = **33.6674 months**, assuming immediate full cash receipt, no fees and no burn change.

A 24-month plan can spend an average **$1,001,592.29/month** net cash, including incremental capex, if all opening cash and the raise are available. No supplied schedule establishes that condition.

A deliberately simple **stress scenario, not a forecast** scales all September opex immediately by headcount 85/38, holds MRR and GM constant, and adds the full raise immediately:

- Opex = $780,000 x 85/38 = **$1,744,736.84/month**.
- Net burn = $1,744,736.84 - $66,010 current gross profit = **$1,678,726.84/month**.
- Post-raise runway = $24,038,215 / $1,678,726.84 = **14.3193 months**.

That sensitivity does not prove the actual forward plan fails. All opex does not necessarily scale with headcount, hiring may ramp, revenue can grow, and capex/financing can change cash use. A linear hiring ramp yields a different answer; it must be named separately. The purpose is to require an explicit monthly plan.

The CSV's burn equals opex minus reported gross profit in all 13 rows. Cash rolls forward by reported burn for all 12 comparable month pairs. No separate capex line is exposed. This is **not** proof hardware costs are omitted rather than embedded in opex/COGS. At the unverified $42,000 hardware assumption, investment/recovery arithmetic would require ownership and financing evidence.

## Evidence gaps and founder questions

1. Which pilots have enforceable commitments, start dates and expected conversion? Provide executed contracts, invoiced MRR and collections.
2. Provide the SKU/site evaluation matrix with denominators, failure classes, autonomous versus assisted success and intervention latency.
3. Supply COGS components: hardware depreciation or lease payments, remote operators, support, maintenance, warranty, cloud inference and field service. Reconcile actual 41% to any target 68%.
4. Supply monthly acquisition cohorts: S&M attribution, new-customer MRR, expansion, contraction, churn, pilot failures and deal lags. Rebuild CAC payback with an explicit methodology.
5. Supply customer-level retention cohorts, exposure periods and logo/revenue churn; n=3 NRR is not broad evidence.
6. Supply a monthly cash model with hiring dates, loaded compensation, working capital, deployments, cash capex, hardware financing, raise fees and a minimum-cash threshold.
7. Supply bottom-up market assumptions, hardware quotes, contract terms and verifiable biographies. These claims are unavailable, not established false.
