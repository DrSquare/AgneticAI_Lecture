## 2. Inventory charges are material, overlapping, and partly reversed

Broad inventory/excess-purchase-obligation provisions were **7,200 vs 3,700**; releases were **1,500 vs 689**. These are rounded disclosures and the broad provisions already include H20 [AR41]. Management reports net margin effects of **−2.6 pp vs −2.3 pp**, an incremental **-0.30 pp** headwind (calc: −2.6 − (−2.3)); this small change is material to a faithful bridge [AR41].

The annual narrative's **4,500** initial H20 charge equates to **2.0839 percentage points** (calc: 4,500 / 215,938 * 100) of annual revenue, **only as a scale illustration** [AR36] [AR51]. It is not the net annual H20 effect or a normalized margin. The more precise quarterly charge was **4,538 in Q1**, followed by a **180 release in Q2**; Q3/Q4 effects were described as insignificant, with exact amounts **not disclosed**, not zero [Q2_RECON] [Q4_RECON]. Do not add the H20 charge to the broad provisions.

Reported GAAP quarterly gross margins recovered **60.5% → 72.4% → 73.4% → 75.0%** from Q1 through Q4 [Q2_SUMMARY] [Q3_SUMMARY] [Q4_SUMMARY]. Standard non-GAAP Q1/Q2 margins were **61.0% / 72.7%**, while separately disclosed H20-adjusted non-GAAP margins were **71.3% / 72.3%** [Q2_RECON]. These are different measures; neither replaces annual GAAP margin.

**Interpretation:** inventory reserves and releases complicate quarter-to-quarter comparisons; later margin recovery does not establish that annual transition costs are fully quantified or permanently resolved. **Unknown:** exact net annual H20 effect and isolated Blackwell margin attribution [AR41] [Q4_RECON].
