## Five key differentiators
1. **Business model split: robot makers vs. robot brains.** Figure, Agility and 1X sell whole robots; Physical Intelligence and Skild sell intelligence that runs on other people's hardware. The model layer has raised faster with fewer assets.
2. **Skild is the only one with meaningful, recurring revenue** ($100M ARR claimed) — achieved by deploying on existing industrial robots (ABB, UR, ex-Fetch fleet) rather than waiting for humanoids.
3. **Agility is the only one with audited financials** — and they show the gap between hype and revenue ($1.78M sales, $138M loss). Its SPAC will set the first public-market price for a pure-play humanoid company.
4. **Target market diverges:** factories (Figure/BMW), warehouses (Agility, Skild), homes (1X), anything with a robot arm (π). Home is the largest prize but the furthest from autonomy.
5. **Data strategy is the moat each is betting on:** Figure (fleet + human video via Brookfield), π (Recap: corrections + autonomous practice), Skild (internet video + simulation), 1X (world model trained on video + teleoperation data from homes).

## Gaining vs. losing ground (last 12 months)
- **Gaining:** **Skild AI** (valuation ~3×, ARR from ~$30M to $100M, acquisition of Fetch team); **Physical Intelligence** (fast model cadence: π\*0.6 → π0.7, first API product).
- **Holding:** **Figure AI** (real production ramp and a second customer, but no revenue disclosure to justify $39B).
- **Under pressure:** **Agility** (public disclosure exposed thin revenue; dependent on SPAC close); **1X** (reported down-round talks, key AI leader departure, no confirmed deliveries).

## Three implications for our investment committee
1. **Underwrite the model layer on deployments, not demos.** Ask model companies for paid ARR and customer logos (Skild's bar), and treat "reported" rounds as unpriced until closed.
2. **Use Agility's SPAC (vote 2 Nov 2026) as a valuation anchor.** Its trading will reset private marks for hardware-first humanoid companies — revisit Figure and 1X comps after listing.
3. **Back picks-and-shovels exposure.** Every company depends on NVIDIA compute, safety certification and real-world data; tooling for robot data, evaluation and safety is a lower-risk way to ride the category.
